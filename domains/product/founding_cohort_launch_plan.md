# Founding Cohort Launch Plan

## Recommendation

Use the interest list as the immediate public CTA, then decide whether the first operating shape should be a limited founding cohort, pilot, beta term, preview term, or another format.

If the next step becomes a limited founding cohort, it should test the strongest parts of the vision while avoiding premature commitments around licensed childcare, public cafe operations, paid teen work, large outdoor play infrastructure, public rentals, and specific pricing.

## Plain-Language Version

Invite homeschool families to join the interest list and help shape Cultivate's first rhythm. The first operating version should prove:

- families want this community
- teens and middle kids respond to the programming
- parents value having a place to stay, work, and connect
- the 5-week rhythm works
- the culture feels safe, inclusive, and real

It should not try to prove every future revenue stream at once.

## Recommended First Operating Shape

### Audience

Homeschool and home-based learning families in Central Florida with:

- teens ages 13-18
- middle kids ages 6-12
- parents who want community, workspace, and support

Families with younger children can be included in discovery, but do not promise Sprout Space childcare until licensing is validated.

### Size

Start with a deliberately small operating group after interest-list validation.

Working hypothesis:

- 8-12 families
- 8-16 teens
- 6-12 middle kids
- parent participation encouraged but not required

This is not an approved capacity. It is a planning target for discussion after interest-list response and facility constraints are known.

### Duration

One 5-week term.

- Weeks 1-4: focused programming and community rhythm.
- Week 5: open/reflection week, family feedback, prospective-family observation, and next-cohort enrollment.

### Days

Start with 2-3 days per week, not full-time.

Working options:

- Option A: Tuesday/Thursday.
- Option B: Monday/Wednesday/Friday.
- Option C: one teen day, one middle-kid day, one shared/community day.

Final schedule should be chosen after founding-family discovery.

## Included In First Operating Version

### The Summit

Teen community and life-skills programming.

Allowed language:

- teen community
- real-life skills
- projects
- communal learning
- inclusive environment
- creative/media/home/life skills

Avoid promising:

- paid teen jobs
- formal employment credentials
- licensed vocational training
- guaranteed academic curriculum outcomes

### The Stepping Stones

Middle-kid hands-on project programming if family demand and qualified staff support it.

Allowed language:

- making
- discovery
- age-appropriate life skills
- creative projects
- bridge experiences with teens

Avoid promising:

- childcare
- school replacement
- therapeutic services
- regulated care

### The Grove

Parent workspace/community area.

Allowed language:

- parent lounge
- co-working style space
- WiFi and work tables if available
- community connection

Avoid promising:

- guaranteed private office pods
- business-class co-working amenities
- always-available workspace

### Shared Workshop Or Gathering Space

Shared room for cohort workshops and gatherings.

Allowed language:

- workshop space
- family gathering space
- community room

Avoid promising:

- public rentals
- private events
- after-hours rental access
- catering

### Culture Artifacts

Low-risk pieces that make Cultivate feel real:

- Village Wall.
- family skills inventory.
- project showcase.
- community moment of the week.
- optional contribution board.
- feedback circles.

## Excluded Unless Validated

- The Sprout Space childcare.
- paid teen childcare roles.
- public Harvest Cafe.
- Foxtail partnership.
- public Commons rentals.
- full Wilds outdoor playground.
- Garden produce going into meals or cafe.
- specific pricing.
- scholarship acceptance before Step Up provider status is confirmed.

## Optional Pilots If Safe

These can be tested lightly if the site and rules allow:

- bring-your-own snack/lunch rhythm.
- simple parent coffee table, not a cafe.
- garden activity using containers or raised beds.
- outdoor free movement in an already-safe space.
- teen-led project showcase.
- parent-led workshop.

## Success Criteria

The founding cohort works if:

- families want to return
- teens feel safe and invested
- middle kids engage with projects
- parents stay and connect
- families contribute without pressure
- the 5-week rhythm feels natural
- operational workload is visible and manageable
- the next version becomes clearer

## Failure Signals

Pause or redesign if:

- families only want drop-off care
- teens do not feel ownership
- parent participation feels forced
- staffing load is too high
- safety/supervision boundaries are unclear
- the model depends on unvalidated regulated pieces
- pricing assumptions cannot cover realistic costs

## Founder Decisions Needed

- What family count feels right for the first cohort?
- Which age group is the anchor: teens, middle kids, or both?
- What days/times should be tested?
- Should younger-child families be discovery-only until childcare is validated?
- What language should be used for the first operating offer after interest-list validation: pilot, founding cohort, beta term, preview term, or something else?
