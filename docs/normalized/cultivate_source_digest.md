## Core Company Concept

Cultivate is a homeschool family campus in Central Florida serving ages 0-18. It is positioned as a complete ecosystem rather than a single drop-off program. The promise is "Real Skills for Real Life."

The concept serves the whole family:

- Little children have childcare and early childhood care.
- Middle kids have hands-on exploration and bridge programming.
- Teens have life skills, community, work experience, and inclusive support.
- Parents have co-working space and community.
- The broader homeschool/local community has rentable shared space.
- Food, gardening, service, and family contribution connect the campus.

## Indoor Campus Components

### The Sprout Space

- Audience: ages 0-5.
- Role: on-site childcare for Cultivate families, also open to broader community as space allows.
- Age groups: infants/toddlers 0-3 and preschool 3-5.
- Staffing: qualified lead childcare educators plus trained, paid teens from The Summit/Glade.
- Safety requirements: Florida DCF childcare licensing, background checks, staff-to-child ratios, waivers, health forms, incident reporting, supervised teen workers.
- Experience: warm, calm, play-based, sensory-rich, secure attachment over structured curriculum.
- Ecosystem connection: lets families participate without separate childcare; creates teen childcare work pipeline.

### The Stepping Stones

- Audience: ages 6-12.
- Role: middle kids community space and bridge between early childhood and teen programming.
- Focus: hands-on making, discovery, early life skills, creative projects, community identity, bridge programming.
- Activities: construction, cooking, art, creative media, science, nature, gardening, tidying, money basics, communication, conflict resolution, caring for others.
- Staffing: facilitators with education, child development, or youth programming backgrounds; Glade/Summit teens may serve as junior mentors.
- Rhythm: gentle gathering, project time, skill-building, outdoor or creative exploration, communal snack/lunch moments.

### The Summit

- Audience: ages 13-18.
- Earlier doc name: The Glade.
- Role: teen community space and heart of the teen life-skills model.
- Focus: safe, unhurried, judgment-free space with life skills, projects, creative arts, communal cooking, core enrichment, and neurodiverse inclusion.
- Daily rhythm: unstructured mornings, community gathering around 11:00am, food and work of the day.
- Weekly rhythm from earlier Glade source: Monday/Wednesday/Friday life skills and projects; Tuesday/Thursday optional core enrichment using Layers of Learning.
- Skills: home repair, cleaning, money management, communication, creative arts and media, cooking, nutrition, budget meal planning, personal expression, identity development.
- Inclusion: staff may include ABA therapy or special education backgrounds.
- Ecosystem work roles: Harvest Cafe, Sprout Space, Growing Grounds, mentorship in Stepping Stones, sign/build projects for The Wilds.

### The Harvest Cafe

- Role: community cafe and connective heart of campus.
- Offerings: coffee, tea, smoothies, pastries, healthy snacks, light bites.
- Learning model: teen-run real business experience, not a simulation.
- Teen skills: menu planning, recipe development, food costing, profit/loss thinking, customer service, communication, food safety, POS operation, transactions, marketing.
- Ecosystem connections: serves campus families, visitors, Commons events; may use produce/herbs from The Growing Grounds; may cater Commons bookings.
- Partnership path: possible local coffee partnership, especially Foxtail, while preserving teen-centered operations.

### The Grove

- Audience: parents, especially remote-working parents.
- Role: parent co-working lounge.
- Features: desk space, high-speed WiFi, private office pods, lounge seating, cafe access, printing, basic office amenities.
- Membership options: monthly membership, day pass, discounted family bundle tied to active Cultivate memberships.
- Ecosystem role: reduces the drop-off/waiting barrier and helps parents stay, work, connect, volunteer, and deepen community.

### The Commons

- Audience: Cultivate families, homeschool co-ops, educators, specialists, therapists, community groups, nonprofits, private event hosts.
- Role: multi-use community space and rentable revenue stream.
- Uses: co-ops, classes, workshops, small events, meetings, information sessions, private parties, celebrations.
- Features: flexible floor plan, tables/seating up to an unspecified guest count, projector/screen, WiFi, prep area, optional Harvest Cafe catering, possible private entrance for after-hours events.
- Booking options: half-day, full-day, recurring, evening/weekend after-hours.
- Pricing: discounted member rates, standard non-member rates, possible reduced co-op/nonprofit community rates.
- Priority: active Cultivate members and homeschool community organizations.

## Outdoor Campus: The Wilds

The Wilds is the outdoor campus and outdoor heart of Cultivate. It is marked by a hand-crafted sign made by teens from The Summit.

### The Sprout Yard

- Audience: ages 0-5.
- Role: safe, sensory-friendly outdoor play space.
- Features: age-appropriate equipment, soft surfaces, calm enclosed environment, sensory-friendly textures/sounds/spaces.
- Supervision: Sprout Space staff.

### The Range

- Audience: all ages.
- Role: shared outdoor play and movement space.
- Features: larger play set, open field, inclusive/adaptive equipment, special needs swings.
- Operation: shared schedule with dedicated outdoor time by program plus cross-age connection.

### The Canopy

- Audience: ages 13-18.
- Role: teen outdoor retreat and outdoor extension of The Summit.
- Features: shade, hammocks, hammock chairs, comfortable seating.
- Experience: unstructured, calm, restorative, especially valuable for neurodiverse teens.

### The Growing Grounds

- Audience: all ages.
- Role: working garden and food-learning loop.
- Purpose: eventually supplies fruits, vegetables, and herbs to The Harvest Cafe and communal meals.
- Learning: plant life cycles, soil health, horticulture, Central Florida seasonal planting/harvesting, responsibility, collaboration, food origins.

### The Garden Guild

- Role: dedicated group of kids and teens stewarding The Growing Grounds.
- Term: members commit for a full 5-week term.
- Daily schedule: 2-3 Guild members tend the garden each day.
- Age model: multi-age, with older kids/teens mentoring younger members.
- Recognition: tracked and celebrated on The Village Wall each term.
- Entry: new Guild members join at the start of each 5-week term during open enrollment week.

## Operating Rhythms

### 5-Week Term

- Weeks 1-4: focused programming, workshops, projects, Garden Guild rotations, core enrichment, life skills, outdoor programming.
- Week 5: intentionally open, unstructured exploration, open house days, prospective family visits, new member enrollment, reflection, celebration.

### Family Contribution

Current final-source model: contribution is invited, not required.

- Weekly contribution: 25% off monthly membership.
- Monthly contribution: 10% off monthly membership.
- Occasional contribution: 5% off for the month contribution occurs.
- No contribution: standard rate, no penalty.

Earlier-source model: every enrolled family contributed volunteer days each semester. Treat the final-source discount/invitation model as current unless the founder decides otherwise.

### Community Fund

Optional fund supported by voluntary financial and supply donations. Purpose: subsidize memberships, support programming, keep Cultivate accessible. No expectation attached.

### The Village Wall

Near the entrance. Celebrates financial donors, volunteers, supply donors, Garden Guild members, project highlights, photos, and community moments of the month.
